Profile

Connecting mechanism discovery with pharmacological intervention through vascular biology.

Dr. Haocheng Lu received his bachelor’s degree in Clinical Medicine from Peking University Health Science Center, together with a dual bachelor’s degree in Economics from the National School of Development, Peking University. He earned his Ph.D. in Physiology from the University of Michigan in 2019 and subsequently completed postdoctoral training in the Department of Internal Medicine at the University of Michigan. He is now an Assistant Professor in the Department of Pharmacology, School of Medicine, SUSTech.

His research centers on aortic aneurysm, atherosclerosis, metabolic dysfunction, and vascular cell homeostasis. The lab integrates human genetics, multi-omics maps, animal disease models, and pharmacological perturbation to link mechanistic discovery with translational validation.

Research

Research Directions

01

Aortic aneurysm mechanisms and therapeutic targets

We investigate aortic aneurysm, a highly lethal vascular dilation disease, focusing on how vascular smooth muscle cells, macrophages, perivascular adipose tissue, and genetic variants jointly drive lesion development and rupture risk.

  • Identified the smooth muscle TFEB-BCL2 axis as protective in abdominal aortic aneurysm and proposed TFEB activation by cyclodextrin as an intervention strategy.
  • Combined GWAS/TWAS with mechanistic studies to reveal the role of TCF7L2 in thoracic aortic aneurysm.
  • Characterized spatially distinct PVAT remodeling in response to AngII.
02

Atherosclerosis and metabolic disease mechanisms

We study how shear stress, endothelial inflammation, lipid metabolism, and glucose metabolism reshape vascular homeostasis, with the goal of identifying molecular nodes connecting cardiovascular and metabolic disease.

  • First demonstrated that endothelial TFEB suppresses inflammation, oxidative stress, and atherosclerosis.
  • Showed that TFEB contributes to angiogenesis, insulin signaling, and systemic metabolic homeostasis.
  • Revealed how lipid-metabolism genes such as TM6SF2 affect cholesterol metabolism and diabetes-related phenotypes.
03

Cardiovascular drug discovery and technology translation

We develop in vivo functional screening strategies and validate therapeutic candidates in disease models, building a translational pipeline from high-throughput discovery to mechanistic intervention.

  • Established CRISPR-MI, an in vivo CRISPR screening platform for identifying disease-driving genes within complex lesions.
  • Integrated scRNA-seq to reveal TREM2 as a regulator of macrophage infiltration and apoptosis and as a potential AAA therapeutic target.
  • Systematically studied the pharmacological potential of nitro-fatty acids in endothelial protection, inflammation resolution, and metabolic disease.
